## v1.9.95 — Phase 6.2: the lifecycle seam closed (2026-08-06)

*Recorded 2026-08-06. **Phase 6 checkpoint 6.2.** Decision **D133** new; findings **389—390**, each with the rule-26 Consequences field. No migration and no schema change: `strategies.config_json` is an existing TEXT column and `strategies.status` carries no CHECK (SCHEMA declares none, deliberately), so `control` needs no DDL. Full suite green at 1,172 tests. Next-free finding after this entry: **391**.*

### finding 389 — the lifecycle seam was two holes, and the second one was invisible

`CandidateFactory` writes a `strategies` row plus a `trials_registry` row and stops; the registry returned null for anything outside three hardcoded ids; `DailyPipeline` logged a warning and skipped. That much was known. **What the 6.2 survey added is that the warning was the LESSER hole**: `DailyPipeline` iterates **ACCOUNTS**, not strategies, and only `DummyRoster` opened accounts — for its three ids. So an admitted candidate with no account was **never reached to be warned about at all**. It could clear the D89 detectability gate, spend a trial from a budget that raises the Bonferroni floor for every candidate after it, and never trade a day, while nothing in any log said so. A fix aimed only at the registry would have closed the visible half and left the silent one. Closed by `StrategyRoster`, **forward only**: a replay opens its own accounts (D37) and generation 2, which the D106/D117 harness reproduces, was built from exactly the roster the replay seeds — opening candidate accounts there would change what trades and risk `FX-RecomputeParity` for no gain, since only a forward track judges a strategy (rule 1). An admitted row this build cannot construct still gets **no** account: an account the funnel skips every day is a worse lie than the missing account it replaced.
**Consequences:** `StrategyRoster` (new) + its wiring in `DailyPipeline`; `StrategyRegistry` replaces `Phase2StrategyRegistry` with one row-driven entry point shared by the daily run and the seeding backtest, so the two can never accept different strategy sets. `RunDay_UnknownStrategyAccount_Skipped_RunStillCommits` is unchanged and still passes — unknown still means unknown.

### finding 390 — a control would have taxed its own arena

`CandidateFactory.CreateCandidate` hardcoded `Status = "candidate"` and added a `trials_registry` row **unconditionally**. D81 rule 4 requires the no-LLM twin to register as a control that spends **no** trial — it exists to price the seat, not to compete — so a twin created through the only available path would have incremented the deflated-Sharpe trials count for **every other strategy in the arena**, raising the D89 admission floor for candidates that had nothing to do with it. The defect had no symptom yet only because the contestant seat does not exist until Phase 6.5; it would have surfaced as a quietly harder gate, attributable to nothing. `CreateControl` now writes `status='control'` with no trial, exempt from the D52 pre-registration gate **rather than smuggled past it with the `unregistered` marker** — that marker means something different (a candidate created without a linked hypothesis) and renders permanently on the strategy card under rule 16. A control is deliberately un-preregistered, not carelessly so. The `SignalRegistrar` doctrine applied: *a registration is NOT a candidate*.
**Consequences:** `CandidateFactory` (a `status` parameter, the conditional trials write, `CreateControl`); the unregistered marker now writes THROUGH D133's shape instead of around it via an options-less `JsonNode`, closing the two-writers-one-key hole D133 names. Fixtures `D81_ControlRegistration_WritesNoTrialsRow` and the control-beside-treatment trials count.

*Verification: `check-register` green at **133 rows, D1..D133**; `dotnet test` green — **1,172 tests**, seven projects; `ci.ps1` green. One behaviour found by a failing test rather than by reading: the seeding backtest's pre-flight runs BEFORE the replay that seeds the legacy roster, so a row-requiring check refused the three dummies for not yet existing — `CanRun` distinguishes "runnable in principle" from "resolvable from a persisted row", and the distinction is documented at both.*

## v1.9.96 — Phase 6.3: the funnel primitives and the population hookup (2026-08-07)

*Recorded 2026-08-07. **Phase 6 checkpoint 6.3.** Decisions **D134** and **D135** new; findings **391—392**, each with the rule-26 Consequences field; proposal **P21**. No migration and no schema change: the cadence-family declaration lives in the existing `config_json` TEXT column, in D133's frozen string bag. Full suite green at 1,196 tests across seven projects. Next-free finding after this entry: **393**.*

### finding 391 — D133's TOLERANT property was documented, not implemented: a stamp discarded every unknown key

`StrategyConfigJson`'s class doc states that a historical payload deserializes to a config *"whose unknown keys are PRESERVED for round-tripping"*. They were not. `Read` populates only the declared members, `StrategyConfig` has nowhere to hold anything else, and `WithUnregisteredMarker` was implemented as `Write(Read(x) with { Unregistered = true })` — so **every key the typed shape does not name was silently dropped at the moment of stamping**. This is not theoretical: `AnalysisEndpoints` accepts a caller-supplied `config_json` and `CandidateSpec` carries it verbatim (its own test fixture is the literal blob `{"lookback":126}`), so a candidate created through the API lost whatever it froze — a frozen param (D17) vanishing at the exact moment of freezing, with no error and no log. Found by 6.2's own `D81_ControlRegistration_WritesNoTrialsRow` when 6.3 routed a second stamp through the same path: the typed round-trip ALSO invents keys, and the fixture's `DoesNotContain("unregistered")` caught the invented `"unregistered":false`. The fixture was right on both counts and neither was relaxed. Both stamps now mutate the object form and re-emit canonically. **D133's framing survives intact:** *"through the shape"* always meant ONE writer under ONE convention — the canonical key order plus `AlphaLabJson.Options`, both still applied — and it never meant a typed round-trip; the typed round-trip is what made it lossy.
**Consequences:** `StrategyConfigJson.WithUnregisteredMarker` (rewritten) and the new `WithFrozen`, both over a shared `Stamp` helper. D133's row is unchanged: the defect was in the implementation of a property the row already claims, so there is nothing to amend — the row was right and the code was not. Fixtures `D133_StampingAMarker_PreservesEveryUnknownKey_RatherThanRoundTrippingThemAway` and `D133_WithFrozen_NeverOverwritesADeclaredValue_BecauseThatWouldBeAnUnrecordedFork`.

### finding 392 — a right-number/wrong-subject citation, which `check-register` structurally cannot catch

STRATEGY_CATALOG §3's tie-break rule cited **(D40; v1.9.23)**. D40 is *versioned append-only bars*; it says nothing about ties, and no register row for the seeded tie-break existed at all. Check 3a verifies that a cited `D<n>` RESOLVES TO A ROW — it cannot verify that the row says what the citing text claims — so the citation passed every sweep while pointing at a different decision. The consequence was not cosmetic: a design rule that lives in exactly one document, under a citation that looks authoritative, went unimplemented for three phases while the shipped code did the opposite of what it required. Two of the catalog's three `D40` citations are CORRECT (§2's watermark note and §13's determinism line, both genuinely D40's subject), which is why a bulk re-citation would have been wrong and each was read before deciding. **The generalisable lesson, recorded rather than guarded:** an automated semantic check is not proposed here — it would need to compare a citation's CLAIM against a row's SUBJECT, which no grep can do. What is available is the discipline rule 25 already states: a rule that binds behaviour takes a register row, and a rule found citing someone else's row is a finding.
**Consequences:** `docs/STRATEGY_CATALOG_v1.9.md` §3 re-cited to **D134**, with the correction stated inline so a reader of the old text is not left wondering. `tools/check-register.ps1` is UNCHANGED and deliberately so — no new check is added, because the check that would have caught this cannot be written.

### The two decisions

**D134 — the Stage-3 tie-break is a seeded stable order over (`Config.Seed`, `security_id`).** The catalog has required it since v1.9.23; the code broke ties by ascending `security_id`, which is ingestion order — one of the three orders the rule names as forbidden. **The evidence that settles the framing:** ties are not a boundary event. `BuyAndHoldModel` scores a literal 1.0 for every eligible name and `ThresholdModel` emits only 1.0/0.0, so the tie-break IS the selection rule whenever the qualifying count exceeds the cap. Measured read-only against the frozen store: `threshold:sma50`'s 60-name cap cut into a block of exact 1.0 ties on **4,789 of 5,031** replay sessions, and its truncation count EQUALS its tie-cut count — every truncation it ever made was a tie cut. On 2020-06-15 the book was the 60 lowest security_ids out of 412 names tied at 1.0. Its 33,332 replay trades are an artefact of ascending integer id, and two siblings differing only in `Config.Seed` drew identically, which makes the seed a frozen param that did nothing. Nor does the problem dissolve when the real families land: `brk:L252` scores exactly 1.0 for every name at its trailing high, which three places in the repo already call ordinary.
**Landed NOW, before the first registration, and that is the decisive reason** — not the defect. No forward day has ever run in this arena (the store holds `run_kind='replay'` only), so nothing live is disturbed; whereas registering Momentum and MeanReversion at 6.10 under the superseded order would make the correction a **fork under rule 8**, spending a trial and raising the D89 floor (N′ = live trials + 1) for every candidate after it. That is rail 1 exactly: *the rails that change what a number MEANS land before the number is produced.* Forward-only scoping was rejected on a second, independent ground: `SeedingBacktestEngine` produces S1's backtest reference under `run_kind='replay'`, so a forward/replay split would corrupt S1 by construction — and 6.2 had just spent D133 making the two consumers agree on WHICH strategies run.
**The cost, priced rather than discovered later.** `FX-RecomputeParity` is NOT the tripwire and never was: it seeds equity curves directly and never reaches the funnel, so it cannot fail on a selection change — the plan's framing rested on that error and it was checked before acting. Generation 2 is **not re-run** (replay is skip-if-committed; only an explicit `--reset` re-simulates, and D95's one-generation guard would delete all 5,031 sessions to do it). What is genuinely lost is bounded and named: the three replay accounts' funnel outputs cease to be RE-DERIVABLE under the new code, and the `allocator_value_add` line in the 2026-08-03 sign-off report — a check D107 kept freeze-gating — stops being reproducible, because `AllocatorValueAddKpi`'s roster reads `threshold:sma50`'s curve (168 of 239 replay `allocation_log` rows carry its weight). Nothing frozen loses INTEGRITY: the four calibrated rows are built from D64 plants, which bypass the funnel entirely, and the report's sha256 still matches on disk. Scoring this change falls OUTSIDE the D106/D117 harness — by that decision's stated RATIONALE (*"those change the trades every later number is computed from"*), not by its parenthetical list, which does not enumerate selection.
**Two coupled sites, not one.** `FunnelRunner.RanksOf` carries the same order and says in prose that it must — *"two different tie orders would let a name be simultaneously inside the top N and past the exit buffer"* — and that invariant had no test, because ascending id gave the two sites no way to disagree. A seeded order gives them one, so it is now a fixture. The mixer moved to `AlphaLab.Core.Numerics.Mix` so the funnel's order and `PopulationRng`'s score source share ONE SplitMix64: the arithmetic is unchanged and `FX-PopDeterminism` proves it.
**The `percentile_rank` convention, settled in the same row.** The catalog uses it as a primitive in four scoring blocks and never defines it. Ruled MID-RANK in [0,1], and the reason is a hard-rule-7 consequence rather than taste: under `(rank-1)/(n-1)` the worst-ranked name scores exactly 0.0 and `Selection` refuses it with *"score 0 is not > 0 — never selectable (rule 7)"* — a reason that would be FALSE, since the name was scored legitimately and merely came last. Mid-rank's minimum is `0.5/n > 0`: last, not excluded. It lives in `AlphaLab.Core` because `AlphaLab.Strategies` does not reference `AlphaLab.Evaluation`, so the existing `Statistics.PercentileRank` was structurally unreachable from the models that need it; rather than copy it, `Statistics` now DELEGATES.

**D135 — a strategy declares its matched population's cadence family; nothing derives it.** Hard rule 9 requires every strategy to be ranked against its turnover-matched population and D36 makes the cadence family the matching unit, but **nothing in the repo mapped a strategy to a family**: `strategies.family` is the STRATEGY family (momentum|meanrev|lowvol), a different namespace, and the pipeline hardcoded the daily cost-on population for every promotable strategy. `CandidateFactory` contained no population code at all, though STRATEGY_CATALOG §5.2 has asserted since v6 that *"the factory wires every new candidate to its matched random population by cadence family"*. DERIVATION was rejected on evidence: only `ScheduledRebalance` carries an interval, so `Never`, `TargetOrTimeStop` and `ChannelExit` would have no answer, and §6.4 resolves Breakout by SPAWNING a family, which no derivation rule can express. ONE resolver replaces three copies of `Family == "daily" && CostsOn` — the last of which, in `BandInputs`, carried a comment asserting the copies must agree while citing a `DailyPipeline` line number that had already moved. The population and the D56 trajectory curves key off the SAME resolved family and move together, because a curve states what a no-edge percentile looks like within THAT family's null. Fail-closed is the catalog's own answer: a declared family with no spawned population leaves S3 `undefined`, never a fall back to the daily null.

*Verification: `check-register` green at **135 rows, D1..D135**; `dotnet test` green — **1,196 tests**, seven projects; `ci.ps1` green. Behaviour on the existing roster is UNCHANGED by D135 (every frozen row resolves to daily by the recorded compatibility rule) and CHANGED by D134 by construction — which is the point, and which is why it lands while the forward ledger is still empty.*
